# Compound (working name)

August 30, 2026. One line: Duolingo for money, where the lesson pay
becomes a portfolio and your streak is time in the market.

Mock: public/sketches/compound.html (playable, phone frame).

## Why Duolingo works, and what maps

- Sessions are three minutes, tap only, and you cannot fail
  expensively. Maps directly: every item is a tap, wrong answers cost
  nothing but show one plain sentence.
- One road, next node visible, locked nodes ahead. Maps: a path of
  units (Shares, Funds, Bonds, Spotting hype, Reading a company).
- The streak is the retention engine, loss aversion on a number you
  built. Here the streak is not decoration: each day you show up, the
  market clock advances. Miss a day and your market waits. The streak
  literally IS time in the market, which is the thesis said as a
  mechanic instead of a sermon.
- Duolingo's dead end: XP is a score you never spend. Here the lesson
  pays fake dollars, and the dollars only have one place to go.

## The loop

- One lesson a day pays $20.
- The market screen is where pay goes to work. Each asset type is
  locked until you finish its unit: you cannot buy a bond you cannot
  explain. Learning is the license to invest.
- Overnight, three sim months pass on baked historical style prices.
  You come back to "your money grew $4" or, some days, to a drop, and
  that day's lesson is about not selling.
- The portfolio is the real scoreboard: line chart with buy dots,
  per holding "you put in $20, worth $24."

## What each part teaches

- Streak advances the clock: time in market beats timing the market.
- Locked assets: understand it before you own it.
- The baked crash around week two: drops arrive after you are
  attached, and bonds hold while stocks fall.
- Guess the Stock items reuse the built game as a lesson type.
- Later units go where Nick asked: P/E in plain words, fees
  compounding against you.

## Honest edges to settle

- Lesson pay is fake money earned by quizzes. Growth on it uses
  real style returns. Is that honest enough, or does it need a
  "practice portfolio" label?
- Sim speed: 3 months a day makes compounding visible in a week but
  is not real time. Alternative: real daily prices, which teach that
  daily moves are noise but kill the visible growth.
- No selling in the mock. A real version needs sell, and panic
  selling should cost, like StackMarket's crash gap rule.
- Bridge to real life: does the graduated player export their sim
  allocation as a starter plan, Jared's "apply to real portfolios"?
