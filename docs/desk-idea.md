# The Desk (working name)

August 28, 2026. One line: sort the day's stock pitches without falling
for the hype.

## How it fits the brief

- Steers away from risky behavior: hype arrives as repeated papers and
  a ticking clock, and the end-of-level score shows what hype buys made
  versus what numbers buys made, in dollars.
- Connects to real stocks: every paper is a real company at a real
  moment. Names are hidden until the reveal.
- Teaches evaluation, Nick's ask: the numbers are printed on the papers
  and in requestable reports. Price to earnings is said in plain words
  first ("you are paying $80 for every $1 it earns in a year") and
  named later.
- Emotional, Jared's ask: the reveals. "That was Amazon, 2001. You
  passed." Nobody forgets that.

## The game

- You sit at a desk. The clock runs 9 to 4. A stack of papers waits.
- Each paper is a headline about one stock, written to convince you.
  Under it: Buy or Pass. If you already own the stock: Sell or Hold.
  Swipe to clear a paper.
- One or two numbers are printed small on each paper. Alarming ones
  are in red ink.
- You can request the full report on any company. It arrives later in
  the day, and the market closes at 4. Waiting for the report is
  safer. Acting now is faster. That tension is the core of the game.
- Trading only happens during market hours. Papers left at the close
  expire. The reveal later shows that most expired papers were fine to
  miss.
- A hyped company appears as many papers. The number of papers IS the
  hype. Coverage is heaviest at tops, which is true in real life.
- Your own holdings get papers too: a scary headline about a stock you
  hold, with Sell right under it.
- Buying happens only from papers: a stock is buyable only while it is
  being pitched to you. Selling your holdings is possible anytime.
- The stack's height shows the market's mood. A manic day is a tower.
  A quiet day is three papers.
- Portfolio view: a layered area chart over time, one colored band per
  holding, band thickness is its value, cash as the bottom band, total
  height is your worth. A pie chart shows the allocation right now.
- A level is a stretch of real market days from one era. At the end,
  the reveal: real names, what actually happened next, and the score
  in two columns: what your hype buys made, what your numbers buys
  made. Plus one line about the expired papers, which usually cost
  nothing.

## What each part teaches

- Papers repeating: hype is repetition. Buying by paper count is
  measurable, and the score reports it.
- Red ink and reports: read the numbers before the story.
- The report delay against the closing bell: urgency is the salesman's
  tool, and waiting is usually free.
- Scare papers about your own holdings: panic selling arrives looking
  exactly like news.
- Expired papers scored at the reveal: missing most opportunities
  costs nothing.
- One bankroll across the level: position sizing, concentration,
  diversification.
- The layered chart: diversification and growth, visible over time.
- The reveal: real companies, real outcomes, regret and vindication.

## Open

- Day length in real seconds, papers per day, report arrival delay.
- The era list and level lengths.
- The name.
