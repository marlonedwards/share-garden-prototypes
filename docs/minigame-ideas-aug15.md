# Minigame ideas, August 15 2026

Recovered from the clean-room scout session (six isolated agents, one learning-outcome territory each, no knowledge of prior builds). Screened against the design laws and the graveyard; eighteen concepts cut to ten survivors plus a bench. The session ended before the interview that was meant to cut this to five build candidates.

All six scouts are home: eighteen concepts total. I screened every one against the laws (no metaphors, known form, honest data, freedom to fail, one lesson) and against everything we've built or killed, cut the collisions (one was our Hold spec wearing a leverage dial), cut the law-benders (a fee game that pays its fee in Tetris garbage rather than dollars, a slider game that risks reading as a retirement calculator), and trimmed overlapping territories to the strongest survivor. Here are the ten, in plain language:

**1. Six Questions** (spotting fraud). Five investment pitches on the table, one is a fraud, and you have six question tokens: audited statements, regulator lookup, who holds the assets, withdraw $1,000. The fraud is never visible in the pitch, only in a failed answer, which is exactly how it works in real life. Daily case, golf par on questions used. Guess Who with your savings on the line.

**2. Sign Here** (fine print). A contract with a huge headline and genuinely illegible 4px terms, a magnifying lens on your cursor, four red circles to spend, then sign or pass. Every charge that later fires draws a hairline back to the exact sentence that caused it. Half the documents are good deals, so reflexive passing loses money too.

**3. Payoff** (debt). Four glass tubes are your real debts, each visibly filling at its own APR: the $500 payday loan at 391% races while the scary $28,400 student loan barely moves. Each month you aim your paycheck stream. Rate-beats-balance stops being advice and becomes something your hands figured out. Solvable daily with a par in months.

**4. The Claw** (inflation). A real claw machine stocked with real goods at real dated prices: 1970, TV $398, Chevy $2,980. Every grab attempt costs a year and every tag reprints with real CPI. There's a savings slot at the real rate, and in the 70s cabinet your balance grows while your buying power dies; the 80s cabinet honestly flips it. Decade shelves to complete.

**5. Patience** (taxes and holding). Solitaire where every card is a real tax lot with a ring that turns gold at twelve months. Selling young lots takes the visible short-term tax bite; buried cards can't be sold, so players invent burying their winners as their own commitment device; wash-sale rules make loss harvesting a real puzzle. Par is buy-and-hold to the dollar.

**6. Due Dates** (cash flow timing). A calendar month where your income equals your bills, and you drag the movable ones. "Left over: $70" sits pinned at the top while the daily balance strip below dives red on the 8th: you can be solvent for the month and broke on the 12th, and overdraft cascades spawn fee tiles you never placed. Zero luck, authored dailies, share card is the green-and-red strip.

**7. Fifty-Two Weeks** (the emergency fund). Minesweeper, unchanged, except the 52 tiles are weeks, the mines are real emergencies (transmission $1,412, root canal $1,100), and hitting one doesn't end the game: it costs money, and shortfalls become card debt whose weekly interest you watch chase your weekly savings. Every board eventually forces a genuine 50/50, and the same click is a shrug at $2,810 and a death at $80. That IS the lesson.

**8. Dividends** (compounding). A merge game where real companies drip real declared dividends as chips; merge chips and feed them back into the share tile and it visibly, audibly drips faster. Score is annual income at the end, not net worth, so the game points you at rate. Quarterly bills force painful cash-outs. Collection of 24 real payers that genuinely play differently.

**9. Forty Years** (time in the market). A three-lane runner: checking 0%, savings 4%, index 7%, your money as a growing column of bills. Early, hopping lanes for a $1,000 pickup is obviously right; late, two seconds out of the index lane costs more than the pickup, and your thumb learns the inversion nobody explains. Red crashes arrive without warning and dodging them loses on average.

**10. The Year** (diversification). NYT-native chip placement: a 6x6 grid of 36 real companies showing real trailing returns, eight chips, place them anywhere including all on the hot row. The year then resolves with strikes along rows (sectors) and columns (a hidden shared trait like leverage), and some cells go permanently black. You can't pick which line gets hit, only how much of you sits on one line. Daily with carry-over streak.

The bench, one line each: Out the Door (guess what a contract really costs, dial and reveal), Take-Home (subscriptions visibly shrink every future paycheck), The Desk (order tickets slide by begging to be tapped, tapping costs), Sweep (Fruit Ninja over a real correlation map), Ten Years (ride real price paths as roads; Enron's road ends at a cliff on the real date).
