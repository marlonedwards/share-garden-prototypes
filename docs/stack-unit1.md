August 30, 2026, expanded to the full scope August 31 (Marlon's

lot selling on the profile shelf). Built in
public/sketches/stack.html. Six lessons, each: one teach card, then

Kept check shapes: SPOT IT, FOLLOW THE DOLLAR, THE WHOLE PILE,

## Word law (settled Aug 31, all four cuts)

- Teach cards are visual-only: one concept word, the picture, one
  line under eight words. No subtitle, no body sentence.
- No footer explainers anywhere. Interactions teach themselves.
- Correct feedback is 2 to 3 words. Wrong answers keep one short
  teaching line with the bear.
- Lesson end is numbers only: accuracy, pay ticking in as bills,
  the buy prompt. Labels of at most three words, no sentences.
- PLAIN LANGUAGE LAW (Marlon, Aug 31, overrides brevity when they
  conflict): no trader shorthand, no euphemism, no cleverness the
  target audience has to decode. "Buyers piling in" and "which lets
  you sleep" are rejection triggers; the model sentence is "More
  people are buying the stock than selling it." Questions carry the
  clarity; feedback stays short but literal.

## Lesson 2: The price of a piece (teach + 4)

- Teach: The price, live ticking chart. "The price is just the
  last trade."
- Spot it live: buyers piling in (rising chart wins).
- Spot it live: sellers winning (falling chart wins).
- The whole pile: Costco vs Apple, price is not size.
- Plain choice: a rising price proves only more buyers.
- Meet: Costco, Coca-Cola.

## Lesson 3: The ride (teach + 4)

- Teach: Volatility, two rides drawn. "The size of the ride, not
  the ending."
- Spot it live: more volatile.
- Spot it: same ending, which lets you sleep.
- Spot it: one company goes broke, which portfolio survives.
- Spot it: steady grower vs spike and crash.
- Meet: Pfizer, Nvidia.

## Lesson 4: Time beats timing (teach + 4 + do-it)

- Teach: Zoom out, one week vs ten years. "Owners live in the
  bottom picture."
- Ride it out: the crash, three behaviors race.
- Spot it: where do owners live (decade vs week).
- Name the strategy: find the steady buyer (dot charts).
- Name the strategy: find the peak chaser.
- Do the strategy: tap 4 buys onto your year, see your average.
- Meet: McDonald's.

## Lesson 5: What you pay for what it earns (teach + 4)

- Teach: The price tag, two identical stands. "Never the price.
  Price against profit."
- Follow the dollar: a $100 sale, tap the owner's cut.
- Follow the dollar drip: same profit, fewer pieces, fatter slices.
- Spot it: priced on profits vs priced on a story.
- Plain choice: the question is always price against profit.
- Meet: ExxonMobil, Johnson & Johnson.

## Lesson 6: The first stack (capstone, teach + 5)

## Profile (rebuilt Aug 31, Marlon's direction)

- Portfolio is a felt table: each holding is a physical side-view
  stack of checker chips (StackMarket's desk, better graphics:
  edge-notched discs, elliptical top cap, company colors). Tag
  above each stack: name and day change. Cash is a banded bill
  stack on the same shelf.
- Tap a stack: it fans out, every piece shows the price it was
  minted at (the piece IS the lot).
- Selling is TAP-TO-PICK (settled over the slider): tap pieces to
  outline them in white, StackMarket's sell marking; the sell
  button totals the picked lots. Full cost-basis control.
- Friends hold mini chip stacks in their rows.
- Worth chip day change is pinned at the day tick, so selling never
  reads as a loss.
