AC Feedback

Jared

On the Orb, August 6
- The orb represents diversification well but not growth and decline; the metaphor must be foolproof for everything it shows: one stock, many stocks, growth, decline, crash.
- Everything in math maps to physical world experience; think bar graph, stack of coins, pizza as pie chart.
- There is no real game loop, you just put in a stock and randomly play; study casual games like Candy Crush that get the loop right.

On coin stacks, August 18 to 19
- Each coin should represent one share of stock, and coin height is that share's price.
- Growth and decline are the coin growing and shrinking, not coins being added or removed.
- Selling converts a share coin into dollar coins of constant unit size.
- Keep it simple, focus on stocks for now; complexity can always be added later.
- Once the core model and representation are established, you can build all kinds of levels and lessons on top.

On the timing games, August 20
- Really likes the new stock market simulation model.
- Extend it visually into something more colorful and enticing; demote the history graph to the bottom third of the screen.
- Explore a few different visual directions; reference Candy Crush, Duolingo, or the Robinhood UI.
- Develop distinct levels: level 1 is cash and one stock, level 2 has three stocks, level 3 has ten.
- The summary at the end of each level is where the learning will happen.
- Showing that they did better or worse than random is key; incorporate a Random Monkey character you place against, so each round says you did X better or worse than Random Monkey.
- AI could offer feedback on the sequence of trades, maybe three bullet point comments so it is easy to consume.

Call, August 27, 12:00 to 12:40 pm
- Maybe design a game where you are pitched compelling companies as you encounter them throughout the game and have to choose.
- Make it exciting, the way people were excited about GameStop; there are evil companies.
- Try to survive and resist the temptation of single stock trading.

August 20

Call, August 27, 12:00 to 12:40 pm
- The original thesis was that investing is not gambling, and the learning outcomes should support that.
- The target is 18 to 24 plus year olds, who need to try trading on a long time horizon before they go into the real world thinking they can trade crypto and prediction markets.
- Through a gamified experience they should realize that is not the case.
- Maybe a fake Robinhood or Kalshi interface.
