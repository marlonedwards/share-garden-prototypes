Here are the deliverables, designed strictly around your principles: systems-driven, real-time, visually intuitive, and brutally honest.

## A. Five game concepts

**1. Spillway**
*   **The core loop (30s):** Water—rendered as physics-based $1, $10, and $100 bills—pours continuously from a "Paycheck" pipe at the top of the screen. The player drags pipes, splitters, and funnels to route the bills into buckets at the bottom. "Rent" and "Groceries" buckets have physical waterlines that constantly drain. Excess bills can be routed to a shiny "Savings" vault.
*   **Why it is fun:** The frantic, plate-spinning flow-management of *Mini Metro* or *Factorio*.
*   **The ONE financial lesson:** Budgeting and Cash Flow. **The exact mechanic:** The mandatory buckets drain at a fixed real-time rate. The Savings vault does not. You must secure the physical flow of baseline needs before playing with the excess. 
*   **How to play badly:** The player routes all their $100 bills to the Savings vault, starving the Rent bucket. The Rent bucket flashes red, physically shatters, and the game instantly ends in eviction.
*   **Real-time element:** The bills flow continuously; expenses drain continuously.
*   **Competitive variant:** Split-screen. You and a CPU neighbor are both trying to fill a "Vacation" bucket. The CPU properly funds their baseline needs and slowly fills the vacation bucket. 

**2. Crop Folio**
*   **The core loop (30s):** A 10x10 soil grid. The player drags seed bags ($10, $50) onto the grid. Weather icons (Sun, Rain, Frost, Locusts) scroll like a marquee across the top of the screen and drop onto the grid. A "Tech" crop grows violently fast in the Sun but instantly dies in Frost. A "Bonds" crop grows agonizingly slow but survives everything. Player clicks grown crops to pop out $100 bills.
*   **Why it is fun:** The satisfying visual growth and harvesting of *Plants vs. Zombies* or *Stardew Valley*.
*   **The ONE financial lesson:** Diversification. **The exact mechanic:** Weather is exogenous and unpredictable. Crops only survive specific weather. Mixing crops guarantees some yield passes through every weather event.
*   **How to play badly:** The player gets greedy, buys 100% Sun-loving crops, and watches them grow huge. A Frost icon rolls in. The entire field instantly turns to ash. The player has $0 and cannot buy seeds. Run over.
*   **Real-time element:** Weather continuously scrolls; crops grow on ticking timers.
*   **Competitive variant:** A shared grid. If your crops die, a CPU neighbor will immediately plant their own seeds on your empty tiles, permanently taking your market share.

**3. The Turnstile**
*   **The core loop (30s):** A giant, pulsing "Market" sphere sits in the center, generating $1 coins every second. The player can drag their pile of coins from the Market to their "Wallet" to buy shiny cosmetic trophies, or drag them back to the Market to generate more coins. Between the two sits a physical metal turnstile. 
*   **Why it is fun:** The tactile, physics-heavy coin-pushing of *Coin Dozer* meets the number-go-up dopamine of *Cookie Clicker*.
*   **The ONE financial lesson:** Patience versus Churn (trading fees/taxes). **The exact mechanic:** Every single time coins pass through the turnstile, physical metal blades slice 2% off the stack, which falls into a literal incinerator. 
*   **How to play badly:** The player constantly moves money in and out of the market, trying to "optimize" their pile. The turnstile blades furiously chop their wealth to dust. They watch their net worth vanish simply from the friction of movement.
*   **Real-time element:** The market pulses and generates yield continuously.
*   **Competitive variant:** A CPU neighbor who simply drops their coins in the Market and takes their hands off the mouse. The CPU’s pile grows exponentially while the frantic player grinds their own pile to zero.

**4. Jenga-nomics**
*   **The core loop (30s):** Player uses a crane to drop blocks, building a tower to reach a height goal. You can drop "Cash" blocks ($10 each) or "Leverage" blocks. Leverage blocks are massive, letting you build twice as fast, but they have a physical flame burning on them. The flame is extinguished by a continuous stream of $1 bills you must manually feed into it from your cash flow.
*   **Why it is fun:** The precarious physics-balancing of *World of Goo* or *Tricky Towers*.
*   **The ONE financial lesson:** Debt and Leverage. **The exact mechanic:** Debt builds assets fast, but requires continuous cash flow to service. 
*   **How to play badly:** The player builds a massive tower almost entirely out of Leverage blocks. Their income stream cannot produce enough $1 bills to feed all the flames. A block overheats, explodes, and the physics engine sends the entire tower crashing down in a ragdoll mess.
*   **Real-time element:** Interest (the flame) burns continuously and must be serviced in real-time.

**5. Priced In**
*   **The core loop (30s):** Five baskets representing companies slide back and forth across the bottom of the screen. At the top, a neon news ticker flashes: "TECH BREAKTHROUGH!" Two seconds later, green reward balls drop toward the Tech basket. The player furiously drags their $10 bills to buy into the Tech basket to catch the balls.
*   **Why it is fun:** Twitchy, reaction-based arcade catching like *Fruit Ninja* or *Kaboom!*
*   **The ONE financial lesson:** The news is already priced in. **The exact mechanic:** The *millisecond* the news ticker flashes, the physical size of the slot to put money into that basket shrinks, and the "entry fee" physically rises. By the time human reflexes allow the player to drop money in, the cost of entry is exactly equal to the incoming reward balls. Net profit: zero.
*   **How to play badly:** The player chases every headline, paying premium entry fees right after the news breaks. Because they are reacting instead of holding, they slowly bleed their starting cash via entry fees.
*   **Real-time element:** Fast-paced falling objects and moving targets.
*   **Competitive variant:** Transparent "Ghosts" of CPU institutional traders. When the news flashes, the Ghost's money instantly snaps into the basket at the lowest price, visually proving to the player that human reflexes cannot beat the market's reaction time.

## B. Five rivalry mechanics

**1. The "Mario Kart" Ghost (Active vs. Passive)**
*   **How it works:** You share a screen with a translucent CPU opponent. You have a complex dashboard of moving charts and can buy/sell at will. The CPU opponent has no dashboard; its avatar just blindly drops $10 into a broad index basket every 5 seconds, like clockwork, regardless of what the charts are doing. 
*   **Why it is fun:** Time-trial ghost racing. You are constantly measuring your twitchy, high-effort performance against a silent, relentless baseline.
*   **The emergent lesson:** Beating a simple, passive dollar-cost-averaging strategy through active trading is incredibly difficult. The CPU's blind consistency almost always wins.

**2. The "Tetris 99" Garbage Lines (Inflation)**
*   **How it works:** You and a CPU are playing a wealth-building game side-by-side. When the CPU successfully compounds their wealth and hits a milestone, they don't attack you directly. Instead, they send "Garbage"—the cost of your baseline rent and grocery meters ticks up by 5%. 
*   **Why it is fun:** The panicked, escalating pressure of *Tetris 99* or *Puyo Puyo*, where your opponent's success physically raises the floor beneath you.
*   **The emergent lesson:** Inflation and purchasing power. If you leave your money in a zero-yield mattress while the rest of the world (the CPU) grows their wealth, the rising cost of living will crush you even if you made no mistakes.

**3. The "Agar.io" Mass Absorption (Risk of Ruin)**
*   **How it works:** You control a portfolio blob floating in a petri dish, eating $1 pellets to grow. CPU blobs do the same. If a CPU blob gets 20% larger than you, they can visually swallow you whole (game over). You can hit a "Margin" button to instantly double your size, but you constantly bleed mass (interest) until you hit it again to repay.
*   **Why it is fun:** The tense, predatory spatial avoidance of *Agar.io* or *Slither.io*.
*   **The emergent lesson:** Leverage magnifies both gains and losses. Over-leveraging to chase a rival might save you temporarily, but if you don't secure real growth to pay the bleed, the interest will shrink you to death.

**4. The "Mario Party" Balloon Pump (Market Bubbles)**
*   **How it works:** An asset in the center of the screen is inflating like a balloon, its dollar value ticking up furiously. You and three CPU opponents are holding down your spacebars to "stay in." If you release, you lock in your cash. The balloon *will* pop, wiping out anyone still holding. The CPUs have hidden, randomized greed thresholds.
*   **Why it is fun:** Shared-pot brinkmanship and push-your-luck mechanics like *Crash* games or *Mario Party* mini-games.
*   **The emergent lesson:** The Greater Fool theory. You aren't playing the intrinsic value of the asset; you are playing the psychology of the other players. If you wait for the top, you are the bag holder.

**5. The "Catan" Land Grab (Opportunity Cost)**
*   **How it works:** A shared map of 20 unowned "yield generators" (windmills). You and a CPU both generate $1 a second. You can buy a small windmill now for $10, or save up for the mega-windmill for $50. But if you sit on your cash hoarding for the $50, the CPU will buy up all the $10 windmills. 
*   **Why it is fun:** The aggressive spatial denial and snowballing territory control of *Catan* or *Starcraft*.
*   **The emergent lesson:** Time in the market. Assets generate cash flow. By hoarding cash and waiting for the "perfect" big investment, you allow rivals to acquire early assets that compound, making it impossible for you to catch up.

## C. Three steals

**1. The Steal: *Kerbal Space Program* (Maneuver Nodes)**
*   **The Skill:** Orbital mechanics and delta-V budgeting (understanding how early trajectory changes require exponentially less fuel than late ones).
*   **The Mechanic:** Dragging a vector node on a flight path and watching the massive, projected parabolic arc shift in real-time.
*   **The Transplant:** A retirement-planning game. You control a rocket trying to land on the "Retirement Moon" moving across the top of the screen. Your fuel is your cash. You have a "Savings Rate" slider (thrust). A glowing dotted line shows your projected financial trajectory over a 40-year timeline. If you throttle up at Age 20, a tiny amount of thrust (cash) bends the arc perfectly to the moon. If you wait until Age 45 to throttle up, you have to burn max fuel, run out of cash, and watch your trajectory fall hopelessly short into the abyss. Teaches the math of compounding visually.

**2. The Steal: *Dark Souls* (The Bloodstain)**
*   **The Skill:** Risk management and overcoming the sunk-cost fallacy.
*   **The Mechanic:** When you die, you drop your unspent currency (Souls). You must decide whether to push forward into danger or return to safety to "bank" them permanently. 
*   **The Transplant:** A real-time maze game where you pilot a ship scooping up $10 and $50 bills. You can fly back to the "Bank" in the center at any time to permanently secure your net worth. However, roaming "Market Correction" monsters wander the maze. If one touches you, you drop all unbanked cash where you died and respawn at the center. Do you push deep into the maze for the $500 bill, risking the $300 you are carrying? Teaches the critical difference between paper gains (unrealized) and locked-in wealth (taking profits).

**3. The Steal: *Overcooked* (Countertop Bottlenecks)**
*   **The Skill:** Workflow optimization and identifying systems bottlenecks.
*   **The Mechanic:** Chopped ingredients piling up on limited counter space because the frying pans are full, bringing the entire kitchen to a standstill.
*   **The Transplant:** An estate-planning/tax game. You are moving physical blocks of wealth from your "Working Years" zone to your "Heirs" zone. You must pass them through "Tax" checkpoints. If you try to shove all your blocks through the default "Income Tax" checkpoint at the last second, they physically jam up the doorway, and the IRS character scoops up 40% of the pile. You learn to slowly filter blocks through the smaller, narrower "Gift Tax Exemption" and "Trust" doors over the entire duration of the round to prevent the bottleneck. Teaches tax efficiency as a literal spatial puzzle.
