Metaphor probe · blocks

The no metaphor metaphor. One rounded glass block per holding, standing on a shared floor against a faint dollar ruler. Height is value, continuously. Nothing is a plant, a coin or a balloon; the only warm thing on screen is the name over your own row. Part one runs the foolproof checklist live, part two is a sixty second level.

Part 1 · The foolproof checklist

Each panel is one claim the metaphor has to survive.
a · One holding growing
Teaches: growth is your block rising smoothly past ruler lines, so more money is literally more block.
b · The same holding declining
Teaches: a loss is your block sinking below the dashed outline of what you paid, and that outline never moves.
c · Five holdings, re sorting
Teaches: blocks slide past each other into size order as values change, so you feel the lead change instead of reading it.
d · A market wide crash
Teaches: when every block sinks at once and the order barely changes, the drop was the market, not your picks.
Replays on its own too.
e · Bankruptcy, not a bad week
Teaches: a short block is still a block and can rise again, a bankrupt one cracks into grey rubble that stays on the floor.
f · The index block
Teaches: an index is hundreds of hairline blocks fused side by side, so any single one wobbling barely moves the top edge.
g · A dividend arriving
Teaches: a dividend is a thin slice sliding off the top of the block into your cash tray, money you keep even if the block never grows again.
h · Buy and sell as physical acts
Teaches: buying carries a slab of value out of your tray into the block, selling carries it back, and the two together never make value appear.
Tray plus block is always $260.
