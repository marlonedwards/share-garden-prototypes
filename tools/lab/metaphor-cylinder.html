Metaphor probe · the segmented cylinder

One glass cylinder is your whole portfolio. Each holding is a colour band, band height is that holding's value, and the top of the cylinder is your total read off the wall ruler. Beside it stands the index: the same column built from hundreds of hairline bands. Everything is drawn live. Part 1 runs the foolproof checklist, part 2 is a sixty second level.

Part 1 · The foolproof checklist

Each panel is one claim the metaphor has to survive.
a · The cylinder growing taller
Teaches: when your holdings gain, the whole column rises up the wall, so growth is a height you can point at.
b · One band thinning, the rest holding
Teaches: one company falling squeezes only its own band; everything you own beneath it stays exactly as thick.
c · Five bands, five thicknesses
Teaches: thickness is how much of your money sits in that company, so concentration is visible without a single number.
Hover a band for its value.
HoldingValueShare
d · A crash shortening the whole cylinder
Teaches: a crash presses every band down at once, so the column drops as one piece and no single company is to blame.
e · A bankruptcy leaving a grey seam
Teaches: a company going bankrupt does not thin out, it collapses to a dead grey seam that stays in your glass forever.
f · The index cylinder beside yours
Teaches: the index is the same column made of hundreds of hairlines, so it climbs calmly while your few thick bands lurch.
g · A dividend sliding out to cash
Teaches: a dividend is a thin slice that leaves the company band and lands in your cash column, still yours, now spendable.
h · Buying and selling as pours
Teaches: buying pours cash across into a coloured band, selling lifts that band out and pours it back, so nothing is created or destroyed.
