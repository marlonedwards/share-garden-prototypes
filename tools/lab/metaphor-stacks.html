Metaphor probe · coin stacks and the skyline

One holding is a stack of coins. Height is value. The portfolio is a skyline you read in one glance. Everything below is drawn live, no images. Part 1 runs the foolproof checklist; part 2 is a sixty second level that makes you feel the loop.

Part 1 · The foolproof checklist

Each panel is one claim the metaphor has to survive.
a · One holding growing
Teaches: growth is coins arriving one at a time on top of what you already had, not a line moving right.
b · The same holding declining
Teaches: a decline lifts coins back off the top, and the stack you keep is still standing underneath.
c · A five holding portfolio
Teaches: size is instantly comparable, so you can see which company you actually own the most of.
d · A market wide crash
Teaches: a crash shortens every stack at once, so it reads as weather over the whole city rather than one company's fault.
Replays on its own too.
e · Bankruptcy, not a dip
Teaches: a bankrupt company does not shrink, it falls over into rubble that never gets back up, unlike the neighbour that merely dipped.
f · The index block
Teaches: an index is hundreds of tiny stacks fused into one wide terrace, so single stacks wobbling barely move the top edge.
g · A dividend arriving
Teaches: a dividend is a coin popping off the top into your tray, cash you keep whether or not the stack grows again.
h · Buy and sell as physical acts
Teaches: buying is carrying coins from your tray onto a stack and selling is carrying them back, so nothing appears from nowhere.
Total coins never change, only where they sit.
