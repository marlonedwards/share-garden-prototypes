Metaphor probe · filling tiles

The portfolio is one panel cut into tiles. A tile's area is how much of your money sits in that company, so the panel re-cuts itself whenever the weights change. A tile's fill level is measured against the dashed line marking what you paid, so growth is visible inside a tile that never moved. Area is weight, fill is performance. Everything is drawn live in one file, no images and no libraries.

Part 1 · The foolproof checklist

Each panel is one claim the metaphor has to survive.
a · Filling past the line
Teaches: a holding worth more than you paid fills past its own cost line and the overflow glows, so a gain is a level you can see without reading a number.
b · Draining below the line
Teaches: a loss is empty headroom left under the cost line, so you can see exactly how far the tile has to refill to get you back to even.
c · Five tiles, re-cutting
Teaches: area is your money's share, so when one company runs the whole panel is re-cut and every other tile quietly gives up floor space.
d · A market wide crash
Teaches: a crash drains every tile at once while the cuts barely move, which is what tells you it is the weather and not one company's fault.
Refills on its own.
e · Bankruptcy, not a drain
Teaches: a bankrupt tile burns dark and shrinks to a scar pinned in the corner forever, while its drained neighbour keeps its floor space and can refill.
f · The index panel
Teaches: an index is hundreds of micro tiles breathing together, so one going dark is a speck while the panel's average brightness is the thing you own.
Cash
g · A dividend leaving
Teaches: a dividend is a droplet that leaves the tile and lands in your cash bar, money you keep even though the tile's level dips by exactly that much.
Cash
h · Buy is a pour, sell is a drain
Teaches: buying pours cash out of the bar into an empty slot and selling drains a tile back into it, so money is only ever moving between two containers.

The whole probe is one file of DOM and CSS transitions. Tiles are laid out with a squarified treemap, the same cut used by D3's zoomable treemap, recomputed on every value change so the panel re-flows instead of jumping. The level's price table is hard coded, no randomness, so the same play always scores the same. Colour is the validated gain and loss pair (#1baf7a and #d03b3b, worst pair deutan ΔE 9.9), and because green sits just under 3:1 on this surface every tile carries a visible percentage and a table view sits under the level.
