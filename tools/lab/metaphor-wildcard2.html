Wildcard 2, the Kite Field

Every holding is a kite you are flying. Height above the field is what it is worth right now, read against one shared ruler of altitude lines. The wind is the market: it moves every kite in the sky at once, and nobody in the game gets to know which way it will blow next. Your job is simply to hold the line.

Why kites beat the orb

The orb hid change because value was area, and a 20% loss looks like almost nothing when it is spread around a circle. Height is a position on a ruler. Two kites at different heights are compared instantly, by a six year old, from across the room. Growth is literally upward motion.

Loss is survivable and visible at the same time: a sinking kite is still on your line. That is the exact feeling a middle schooler needs about a down market.

What it buys us

Permanent failure is a snapped line. The kite tumbles away and the string goes limp on the ground. Nothing about that looks like "low", so bankruptcy can never be confused with a bad week.

The index is one enormous box kite sewn from hundreds of small panels. The same gust that whips the little kites around barely rocks it, which is the entire lesson in one picture.

Nothing predicts. You cannot steer wind. The only skill the metaphor offers is letting out line and not panicking, which is exactly the behavior we want to teach.
